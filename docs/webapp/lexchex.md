# LeXcheX — get accredited

Many private investment rounds are open only to **accredited investors**.
**LeXcheX** lets you prove that status — using your onchain wealth — and
carry it with you as a credential that other cyberCORPs apps can check.

## Why you might need it

If a [cyberRAISE](cyberraise.md) round (or another offering) is restricted to
accredited investors, you'll need a valid LeXcheX credential before you can
invest. Getting one is a one-time process; once you hold the credential, any
round that requires it can verify you automatically.

## What the credential is

A LeXcheX credential is a **non-transferable certificate bound to your
wallet** — a "soulbound" NFT. It cannot be sent, sold, or moved to another
wallet. It simply lives in the wallet that earned it and proves that wallet's
holder is accredited.

## The process: three steps

### 1. Questionnaire

Tell LeXcheX whether you are applying as an **individual** or a **legal
entity**, and complete the compliance questionnaire (based on the SEC's
accredited-investor rules).

### 2. Evaluation

Connect and verify the wallet addresses that hold your assets. You prove you
control each wallet with a signature, and LeXcheX values the portfolio across
them. The thresholds reflect the accreditation rules — broadly, a net worth
target of around **$1M for individuals** and **$5M for entities**. You can
combine multiple wallets to meet the threshold.

### 3. Sign and mint

Sign the LeXcheX agreement from your wallet. LeXcheX then mints your
accredited-investor certificate to that wallet.

## After you have the credential

* It stays in your wallet. Restricted rounds can check it automatically — you
  won't have to repeat the process for each one.
* It is tied to the wallet that holds it. If you invest from a different
  wallet, that wallet won't carry the credential.
* Credentials can expire or be updated; if a round says yours is no longer
  valid, re-run the process to refresh it.

## Good to know

* **Onchain wealth counts.** LeXcheX exists specifically so that assets held
  in your wallet can support accreditation — something traditional processes
  ignore.
* **The credential is verifiable but not revealing.** Apps can confirm you're
  accredited without seeing your financial details.
* This is **not legal or financial advice.** Accreditation rules vary and
  change; LeXcheX helps you demonstrate status, but whether you qualify is a
  legal question.
