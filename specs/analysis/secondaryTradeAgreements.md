# Secondary Trade Agreement Structure

Analysis of how `SecondaryEscrow`, `Offer`, and `CyberAgreementRegistry` agreement IDs relate in the secondary trade flow.

---

## Two Agreement IDs, Two Purposes

Every secondary trade involves two distinct agreement IDs:

- **`offerAgreementId`** — generated by DealManager at `postOffer()` as `keccak256(abi.encode(offeror, templateId, salt))`. A DealManager-internal key for the `Offer` struct — **never registered in `CyberAgreementRegistry`**.
- **`settlementAgreementId`** — created fresh at each `acceptOffer()` via `createContract()` with both parties known. A fully-signed bilateral agreement, specific to that accepted lot.

The `offerAgreementId` is **never a registry record**. `postOffer()` makes no registry call. At `acceptOffer()`, a brand-new bilateral agreement is created directly with `createContract()` + `signContractWithEscrow(offeror)` + `signContractFor(acceptor)`, keyed by a unique `settlementAgreementId` derived from `offer.salt` and `offer.unitsAccepted` (which acts as a per-offer acceptance nonce).

## Why Two Structs: `Offer` vs `SecondaryEscrow`

This resonates with the ["one offer -> many agreements" issue](./conditions.md):
- one offer -> one `Offer`
- many agreements -> many `SecondaryEscrow`

`Offer` and `SecondaryEscrow` are separate because they have different scopes and a one-to-many relationship:

- **`Offer`** (keyed by `offerAgreementId`) — created once at `postOffer()`, tracks the offer's total state: cumulative fill (`unitsAccepted`), `status` (`LIVE → PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED → FULLY_ACCEPTED`), and all offeror-side parameters.
- **`SecondaryEscrow`** (keyed by `settlementAgreementId`) — created at each `acceptOffer()`, holds settlement-specific routing and ownership-change data: payment destination (`sellerAddress`), fee routing (`feeDestination`), reservation ID to release on void, and `dealMetadata` for `IssuanceManager.secondaryTransfer`.

A single sell offer can generate multiple `SecondaryEscrow` records through partial fills. Each is an independent settlement keyed to its own `settlementAgreementId`, paired with a `LexScrowStorage.Escrow` for asset custody.

`SecondaryEscrow` and `LexScrowStorage.Escrow` are also deliberately separate: `Escrow` is the generic asset-custody layer used across the whole system; `SecondaryEscrow` is the secondary-trade-specific layer that tells the system what to do when the escrow settles or is voided.

## Partial Fill Mechanics

Partial fills (sell offers only — bids are all-or-nothing) work as follows:

1. `postOffer()` reserves units on the seller's cert and stores the `Offer` struct. No registry call.
2. Each `acceptOffer()` computes `settlementSalt = keccak256(offer.salt, offer.unitsAccepted)` — unique per acceptance because `unitsAccepted` is the fill cursor before this lot. Then calls `createContract()` with both parties, `signContractWithEscrow(offeror)`, and `signContractFor(acceptor)` to create a fully-signed `settlementAgreementId`. Each settlement gets its own `LexScrowStorage.Escrow` (for payment) and `SecondaryEscrow` (for routing/metadata).
3. Consideration for partial fills is computed pro-rata: `offer.consideration * params.units / offer.units`.
4. The parent `Offer` accumulates fill (`unitsAccepted += params.units`) and updates status. Multiple accepted lots can be in flight simultaneously, each settling independently.

## Spec Alignment (§7.3.2)

The spec (§7.3.2) originally described the binding-offer model as:

> "the acceptor's signature at acceptance attaches to and completes signing on **the same agreement record**... transitions the agreement out of open-to-matching to fully signed."

The implementation resolves this by deferring agreement creation entirely to `acceptOffer()`. No open-to-matching agreement exists in the registry; the agreement is created bilaterally at acceptance time with both parties' identities known. This aligns with the spec's description of acceptance as the contract-formation event, and naturally supports partial fills without any cloning mechanism — each acceptance creates an independent, fully-signed bilateral agreement.
